What does this tool do?

The hardest part of writing a blog post is deciding what to write about and in what order. This tool reads your topic, the keywords you want to rank for, and your target audience, then produces a structured outline — H2 and H3 sections with bullet points under each one, plus a suggested meta description. Paste it into Google Docs, Notion, or your CMS and start writing.

How to get the best outlines

  • Be specific in the topic. "How to start a podcast for beginners" produces a better outline than "podcasts."
  • Include 1-3 target keywords. The model will weave them into H2 and H3 titles where they fit naturally.
  • Describe your audience. "Small business owners who've never blogged" produces different structure and tone than "experienced developers."
  • Use Detailed for pillar content. Long-form guides benefit from 7-9 sections. Use Brief for weekly posts or short updates.

Frequently asked questions

Is this tool free?

Yes. It runs on Cloudflare Workers AI with a free daily quota. Normal use won't hit limits.

Will this write the whole article for me?

No, and that's intentional. This tool produces an outline, not the article. Writing the actual content is where your unique perspective matters most. The outline gives you a structure to fill in.

Is the outline SEO-optimized?

Partially. The tool weaves your target keywords into section titles where they fit naturally, and generates a meta description. But real SEO requires backlinks, topical depth, and content that satisfies search intent — the outline is just the starting point.

Can I use this for YouTube scripts?

Yes. The structure works well for video scripts — each H2 becomes a major segment, each H3 a talking point. You may want to ignore the meta description section for video content.

Is my input stored?

No. Your topic, keywords, and audience description are sent to the AI model for processing and discarded immediately after. Nothing is stored, logged, or used for training.

Note: Always review and adapt the outline. AI doesn't know your audience or your unique angle — you do.
